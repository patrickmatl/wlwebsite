**To choose a graphic design company in Pretoria, check nine things before you sign anything: real portfolio work, a written revision policy, file handover in open formats, a fixed itemised quote, clear ownership of the final artwork, how long they have been trading, whether the work is templated or custom, realistic timelines, and honest claims.** Any studio that fails two or more of these checks will probably cost you more in redone work than you save on the quote.

We are a design studio ourselves — WL CreationX has been designing professionally in Pretoria since 2013 — so treat this as the checklist we would use if we were on your side of the table. It is the same standard we hold ourselves to, and it applies whether you brief us or someone else.

## 1. Can They Show You Real, Recent Work?

Ask for a portfolio and look for range: logos, print, digital, and ideally work in your general industry. Be wary of portfolios that are all mockups on stock photos with no finished, in-use pieces — mockup templates are downloadable, and anyone can fill them. A studio should be able to walk you through a few projects and explain the thinking, not just show pretty images. You can browse our own work on our [project showcase](/project-showcase-pretoria).

## 2. Is the Revision Policy in Writing?

This is where cheap quotes turn expensive. Before you commit, get three answers in writing: how many revision rounds are included, what a "round" means, and what extra rounds cost. For reference, we include two free revision rounds in every design price and charge R520 per additional revision — stated on our [pricing page](/pricing/graphic-design-pretoria) before you ever speak to us. If a designer cannot answer this question crisply, expect friction later.

## 3. Do You Get the Files in Open Formats?

The single most important contractual point in a design engagement. When the project ends, you should receive final artwork in open file formats — editable source files, not just a flattened JPEG or PDF — along with your logo colour palettes, colour codes and fonts. Without these, you are locked into that supplier for every future edit, and you will pay a new designer to redraw everything from scratch. Ask the question explicitly, because some agencies (including discounted package deals — ours included, which is why we say so on the page) exclude native files unless agreed. The point is not whether files are included by default; it is whether the company tells you the truth about it upfront.

## 4. Is the Quote Fixed and Itemised?

A professional studio quotes the full job before starting: deliverables, number of concepts, revision rounds, file formats, and price. "It depends, we bill hourly" is fine for genuinely open-ended work, but for a logo, brochure or identity pack there is no excuse for not fixing the price. Published pricing is an even better sign — it means the company charges every client the same rate rather than sizing up your budget on the call.

## 5. Custom Design or Templates?

Ask directly: "Is this designed from scratch, or adapted from a template?" Template-based logos are why two businesses sometimes end up with near-identical branding — and why trademark registration can fail. Custom work costs more per concept, but it is yours alone. We design every concept from scratch and put "we do not use templates" in our package descriptions precisely because so much of the low end of the market does.

## 6. Local, Remote, or Both?

You do not need a designer in your suburb, but you do need to know how they work. On-site briefings, print checks and photography obviously favour someone local; pure digital work travels fine. Our only office is in Waterkloof Glen, Pretoria — we work on-site across Gauteng and serve the rest of South Africa remotely, and we say exactly that rather than pretending to have branches in every city. If a company's website lists offices in five cities, ask for the street address of the one nearest you and see what happens.

## 7. How Long Have They Been Trading?

Longevity is not everything, but a studio that has survived a decade of South African economic weather has repeat clients, and repeat clients are the most honest review system there is. Check how long the company has actually been operating — we started designing professionally in 2013 — and be sceptical of "leading agency" claims from businesses registered last year. You can read more about our history on our [about page](/about-graphic-design-company-pretoria).

## 8. Is There a Real Process and a Named Contact?

Good design engagements have structure: brief, concepts, feedback, refinement, handover — with one person accountable for your project. Ask who your contact will be and what happens at each stage. We assign a project manager to every job for exactly this reason. If your emails will go to a generic inbox and be answered by whoever is free, deadlines drift.

## 9. Watch for the Red Flags

In our experience, these are the warning signs that reliably predict a bad outcome:

- **Guaranteed rankings or "page 1 on Google" promises.** Nobody can guarantee rankings. A design company promising them is telling you how it treats the truth.
- **No written quote, or a quote that grows.** Hidden "file release fees", surprise revision invoices, or charges for the fonts in your own logo.
- **Pressure tactics.** "This price is only valid today" is a sales technique, not a design service.
- **No straight answer on ownership.** If they cannot tell you plainly who owns the final artwork, assume it is not you.
- **A portfolio that cannot be verified.** Work presented with no context, or claims of big-name clients with nothing to show for it.
- **Unbelievably low prices.** A R300 "complete branding package" is a template with your name typed into it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I ask a graphic design company before hiring them?

Five questions cover most of the risk: How many original concepts do I get? How many revision rounds are included and what do extras cost? Do I receive editable source files at handover? Is the quote fixed? And who owns the final artwork?

### Is it better to hire a local Pretoria designer or work remotely?

For purely digital deliverables, either works. For print work, signage, photography or anything needing on-site time, local is a genuine advantage. We handle on-site work across Gauteng from our Pretoria office and serve clients elsewhere in South Africa remotely.

### How much should graphic design cost in Pretoria?

As a benchmark from our own published rates: logos from R2,080, corporate identity packs from R4,160 and flyers from R780. Full brand identity packages are quoted on request — as a guide, recent packages have started around R6,683. If a quote is dramatically below that range, check points 3 and 5 on this list very carefully.

### Who owns the design once I have paid?

That depends on the contract, which is why you must ask. The professional standard is that final artwork and usage rights transfer to you on final payment.

### What is a reasonable timeline for a logo or identity project?

Expect a few weeks rather than days for custom work: briefing, concept development, your feedback rounds and final artwork each take real time. Same-day logo promises almost always mean templates.
